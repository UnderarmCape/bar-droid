# Give Claude text eyes and touch hands

**Build approach 2 and run approach 5 inside it.** A dev-only, read-only LuaUI widget exports the game as compact text (own units, factory queues, enemies in line of sight, free metal spots, recent events, and the screen rectangles of the dock and camera controls in display pixels) and pauses the game on the frame an event happens; a laptop executor plays Claude's macro decisions as touch skills through one persistent uinput pipe with finger-like timing and noise, and Claude is woken only when an event or a finished plan step needs a decision. It is the only option that meets both of the owner's standing rules (every order through the app's real touch controls, every BAR run on the dev phone), and it copies the design behind every published LLM win over strong RTS AIs: **the same model fell from 92% to 0% against level-3 StarCraft II AI once it had to pick positions itself**, while the 2025 systems that beat level 7 (HIMA 82%, MASMP 60%) pair a slow planner with scripted execution. Screenshot-only play (1) stays as scheduled "blind probes" of whether the controls can be found and read, because a full frame costs about **3,237 image tokens** and picking small targets from pixels is unreliable. Giving our team to an AI (3) skips touch entirely, and a shadow AI (4) cannot be told apart from touch-issued orders without engine patches and issues per-unit orders that no player gives through a dock. Exact coordinates do not spoil the UX findings if the executor adds finger noise and human timing, logs what the touch layer decided for every gesture, checks control sizes from the exported rectangles, and runs some flows without them. New Beginning is an anti-air race before it is a siege, and the BARb ladder is a race for metal, because Easy, Medium and Hard differ in expansion caps, build delays and build power rather than cheats. Whether a win at the scenario's Beginner difficulty counts is left open for the owner.

## Published agents win by choosing macros, not pixels

Every LLM agent that beats built-in RTS AIs at mid-to-high levels gets the same setup: a compact text state, a menu of macro actions, and scripts that handle placement and micro. TextStarCraft II gave the model a six-category text observation (resources, units, buildings, work in progress, enemy status, research) and a dictionary of macro tokens such as `<BUILD PHOTONCANNON>`, and left micro to rule-based Python ([arXiv 2312.11865](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11865v3)). The sharpest evidence comes from LLM-PySC2. It ran the same GPT-4o-mini with and without "Easy" modes in which the environment chooses build spots and unit control. With both helpers the model won **92% against level 3; with either control or building placement left to the model it won 0%** ([arXiv 2411.05348](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05348)). The failures it lists will look familiar to anyone who has watched a screenshot agent play BAR: screen and minimap coordinates confused, buildings put in invalid or dangerous spots, allies mistaken for enemies, idle units. The lesson for BAR Droid is that Claude should decide "a Chainsaw next to the fusion", and code should work out the spot and the taps.

The winning systems also call the model rarely and plan far ahead. HIMA made **about 11 LLM calls per 20-minute game at 22.5 s each, without pausing**, and replanned only when a sequence failed or more than 10 enemy units came into view. It won 82% against level 7 as Protoss versus Zerg; TextStarCraft II made 636 calls and HEP 468 for much weaker results ([arXiv 2508.06042](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06042v1)). The arithmetic on TextStarCraft II is revealing. 636 calls at 12.1 s add up to 7,695 s of model time inside a 1,200 s game, which is only possible with stepped or paused play. Most published "beats level 5–7" results are therefore paused-play results, and pausing is the field's norm, not a shortcut. MASMP kept decisions consistent from one cycle to the next with three pieces: a state machine whose transitions are written in plain language, a behaviour tree to carry out its actions, and a small memory of strategy variables re-injected every cycle. With them it lifted its baseline from **0% to 60% at level 7** ([arXiv 2510.18395](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18395v1)). The planner model matters most at the top. In HIMA, the level-7 win rate was 90% with GPT-4o and 70% with Claude-Sonnet, but those were 2024–25 models, and no RTS paper tests a current Claude. For real-time pressure, AgileThinker ran a reactive thread beside a slow planning thread. In Overcooked against the wall clock it scored 0.89, against 0.57 for the reactive agent alone and 0.00 for the planner alone ([arXiv 2511.04898](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04898v1)). In BAR Droid, the executor's reflex rules play that reactive role.

Screenshot-only agents do badly in real-time games even when the game waits for them. On VideoGameBench the best model finished 0.48% of the full benchmark and 1.6% of the "Lite" variant, which pauses for the model ([arXiv 2505.18134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18134v2)). Cradle paused Red Dead Redemption 2 and Stardew Valley while it thought, and still failed at precise positioning, combat timing and unfamiliar icons ([arXiv 2403.03186](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186)). The best specialist grounding model found small targets on high-resolution screens only **18.9%** of the time on ScreenSpot-Pro at release ([arXiv 2504.07981](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07981v1)). On Android, Claude Code (Opus 4.7) driving the phone over adb with text and no screen scored 71.8% on AndroidWorld, against 69.3% for the best GUI agent, in 10.7 steps per task instead of 18.6 ([arXiv 2606.19388](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19388.md)). That paper's CLI failures cluster in weak verification, and Minitap's table shows that removing post-action validation cost 15 points ([arXiv 2602.07787](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07787v1)). Anthropic's own Pokémon harness used a model-managed knowledge base of about 8,000 tokens and a "Navigator" that walks the character to a point the model chooses ([ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/building-and-deploying-a-pokemon-playing-llm-agent-at-anthropic)). No LLM agent for Spring, Recoil or BAR turned up, and neither did any agent driving an RTS through touch controls. The nearest UX-agent work, such as UX-LLM on iOS screens, still relies on expert review to confirm what it finds ([arXiv 2411.00634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00634v2)). BAR Droid can borrow the architecture, but there is no benchmark to copy.

## Only approach 2 is both touch-faithful and fast enough

| Approach | Every order a real touch? | Cost per decision | Prospects against BARb Hard | Value for touch UX | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Screenshots with pause/run cycles | Yes | ~3,237 image tokens per full frame, 1–2 s per PNG capture, model guesses pixels | Low: screenshot-only game agents score near 0–2% | Realistic discovery, but agent errors and UI errors are mixed together | Keep only as blind probes |
| (2) Text-state widget, touch skills, event-driven auto-pause | Yes | A few thousand text tokens per strategist call, images on demand, gestures in milliseconds | Best available: the planner-plus-executor design behind every published level-7 win | Rich: touches and retries per intent, touch-layer decisions, size audits | **Build this** |
| (3) An AI plays our team | No | Near zero | High, but it is BARb against BARb | None | Ruled out by the touch-only rule |
| (4) Shadow AI replayed as touch | In principle | Engine/BARb patches, plus a per-unit order stream to translate | Poor: BARb's frame-by-frame edge is lost in replay | Distorted: orders no player gives through a dock | Defer; at most an advisor |
| (5) Slow game speed instead of pause | Yes | Same as the approach it runs inside | Slightly worse than pause during long thinking | Better: units keep moving under the fingers | Use as a mode inside (2) |

### Screenshot loops pay 3,237 tokens to guess a pixel

The current loop pays three costs at once. The first is capture. `screencap -p` spends almost 90% of its on-device time encoding PNG: in one emulator test, a median 2,122 ms per frame against 12 ms for a 768-px JPEG made by an on-device helper ([handsets.dev](https://handsets.dev/blog/category/performance/)). The second is tokens. Claude charges ceil(w/28) × ceil(h/28) visual tokens ([Claude docs, Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), so a full 2316×1080 landscape frame costs **83 × 39 = 3,237 tokens**, while a 768-px frame costs 364 and a 400×400 crop 225. The third is grounding. Claude returns coordinates in the resized image's frame, so a careless rescale shifts taps ([Claude docs, Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), and the grounding literature above shows how often small targets are missed even with perfect scaling. Screenshots alone do have one real strength. Like a person, the agent must find and read the button, so discoverability problems surface. That is why approach 1 survives as a scheduled probe inside approach 2 rather than as the main loop.

### An AI on our team wins without touching the screen

Approach 3 works mechanically. In singleplayer, `/aicontrol 0 BARb stable` passes the engine's permission check (`weAreLeader || singlePlayer || ...`), and BARb then commands every unit on team 0 through `NETMSG_AICOMMAND`, which never touches the screen ([UnsyncedGameCommands.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Game/UnsyncedGameCommands.cpp); [AICallback.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/ExternalAI/AICallback.cpp)). A win produced this way says nothing about the dock and breaks the owner's rule that every game action goes through the app's touch controls, so it is excluded as a way of playing.

### A shadow AI's orders cannot be heard cleanly or replayed in time

Approach 4 fails on separation, translation and strength. A synced gadget's `AllowCommand` does see BARb's orders and can drop them. But when the host's AI runs on the host's own team, its orders reach `AllowCommand` with the same playerID, fromSynced and fromLua values as the human's ([CommandAI.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Sim/Units/CommandAI/CommandAI.cpp); [SelectedUnitsHandler.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Game/SelectedUnitsHandler.cpp)). The engine's own comment says `NETMSG_AICOMMAND` carries orders "sent by AICallback / LuaUnsyncedCtrl", so LuaUI widget orders travel the same way. The server-side `NoHelperAIs` switch drops every `NETMSG_AICOMMAND` ([GameServer.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Net/GameServer.cpp)). It would therefore also drop the app's own widget-issued dock features, such as AUTO RETREAT, which gives its orders with GiveOrder ([CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/bar-droid-app/CLAUDE.md)). That conclusion is drawn from the code path, not tested. A dropped order is never reported back to BARb, so it would keep seeing idle units and issuing new, slightly different orders instead of one clean stream. BAR Droid builds the engine from source, so it could patch BARb to tag its orders. Even a clean stream, though, is a per-unit, frame-by-frame order flow. Replaying it one gesture at a time would strip away the reactivity that makes BARb strong, and it would test the dock with orders no human gives through one. A Lua planner gadget is easier to separate: its orders arrive with playerID -1 and fromLua true, and it can publish suggestions without ever issuing them ([LuaSyncedCtrl.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Lua/LuaSyncedCtrl.cpp)). It needs Lua dev mode, whose `/devlua` command is cheat-flagged and "can cause desyncs" ([SyncedGameCommands.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Game/SyncedGameCommands.cpp)), and SimpleAI-class logic is far weaker than BARb Hard. Its best later use is as an advisor that feeds build-order hints into Claude's prompt.

### Slow motion is a mode, not a replacement for pause

Approach 5 is available as-is. The default speed range is **0.1×–20×**, and `/setspeed` sets any value inside it. `/setminspeed` has no lower bound in code, but going below 0.1× is untested ([GameSetup.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Game/GameSetup.cpp); [GameServer.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Net/GameServer.cpp)). Pause, though, is the better tool for thinking. **In singleplayer, orders given while paused are accepted**: BAR's "Paused is paused" gadget exits at load in singleplayer, and the engine applies command packets during a pause ([cmd_paused_is_paused.lua](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/luarules/gadgets/cmd_paused_is_paused.lua); [NetCommands.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Net/NetCommands.cpp)). Widgets still get `Update` while paused but not `GameFrame` ([Game.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Game/Game.cpp)), so the exporter must write from `Update`. Even at 0.1×, a 20-second Claude call lets 2 game seconds pass, enough for Wasp gunships at speed 159 to cover about 320 elmos. The right mix is to pause for every strategist call and let the executor run at 1× for routine work. During big fights the game drops to a slower speed so the reflex touch sequences keep up, and scheduled 1× windows measure how the controls hold up under real time pressure. This assumes pause and speed are the test harness's clock, not game actions; the owner can rule otherwise.

## Build input, then state, then skills: a seven-step plan

The order follows the dependencies. Every later piece needs gestures that are fast and finger-faithful and a state dump it can trust, so those come first. All game runs happen on the dev phone. The laptop runs only the executor, a Python process kept in the repository's `tools/` with the toolchain's portable Python. Claude's loop becomes "wait for the next event, read the dump, decide, hand a plan to the executor".

| Step | What to build | Existing pieces to reuse | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gesture layer: one long-lived uinput pipe; tap with a set duration and noise, hold, drag, pinch, three-finger | the app's uinput touchscreen; `tools/dock-phone-test.ps1`'s real taps | a real and a synthetic tap take the same branches in `RtsTouch`; ms per gesture measured over USB and Wi-Fi |
| 2 | On-demand capture: on-device JPEG at 768–1176 px plus full-resolution crops | `toolchain.ps1` `Invoke-AppScript` | frames in tens of ms; coordinates rescaled in code |
| 3 | State exporter (dev-only, read-only): dump, events, auto-pause, control rectangles in display pixels | `tools/dev/bardroid_fps.lua` as the pattern; `bardroid_camctl.txt`, `bardroid_place.txt`, the dock file's rects; `WG.resource_spot_finder` | the dump updates while paused; rectangles line up with taps; fog of war respected |
| 4 | Touch skill library and executor: verify after each skill, abort on failure, detect loops, reflex rules | dock, factory-preset, walls, camctl and attack-ping phone tests | each skill passes a scripted drill in a skirmish |
| 5 | UX logger and automatic checks | the touch log `RtsTouch` keeps; `PerfLog` | each gesture row joined to the touch layer's decision and the game's result |
| 6 | Strategist loop: event-triggered calls, compact diffs, strategy memory file | — | a whole game against a passive opponent with no human input |
| 7 | Crash resilience: periodic saves, resume | `tools/overgame-phone-test.ps1`'s Load Game path | a game resumes after a forced kill |

**Step 1 has to put touches in below the gesture classifier.** `adb shell input tap` sends DOWN and UP with the same timestamp, pressure 1.0 and size 1.0, a zero-millisecond touch no finger makes ([AOSP InputShellCommand.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/HEAD/services/core/java/com/android/server/input/InputShellCommand.java)). Android's `uinput -` reads commands from stdin and schedules `delay`s against a time base, so drift does not build up. Its device registers asynchronously, so commands sent before it is ready are lost, and it disappears at EOF ([AOSP cmds/uinput README](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/HEAD/cmds/uinput/README.md)). A warm on-device channel answered in 2–3 ms in one published test, against 40–80 ms for `input tap` ([handsets.dev](https://handsets.dev/blog/tapping-android-in-5ms-vs-appium-uiautomator2/)). Some existing phone tests are "driven through the touch log", which feeds the Lua side directly ([CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/bar-droid-app/CLAUDE.md)). The agent must instead use the real-tap path the dock test uses, so that `RtsTouch`, the code under test, classifies every gesture. Logging the MotionEvent fields of one real finger tap and one synthetic tap, then diffing them, is a one-time check that the synthetic touches reach the same code branches.

**Step 2 skips scrcpy at first.** Its stream claims 35–70 ms latency, but it adds a hardware encoder and needs the server version to match exactly ([scrcpy README](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy); [scrcpy develop.md](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy/blob/master/doc/develop.md)). That is extra load on a phone already short of memory. An on-device JPEG helper made 768-px frames in 12 ms ([handsets.dev](https://handsets.dev/blog/category/performance/)). scrcpy can come back later, with `--max-fps` and `-m` caps, only to record MP4s for the UX log. Since the dev phone runs with Extra dim and minimum brightness, one check is needed that captured frames are not darkened.

**Step 3 is the load-bearing piece.** BAR's sandbox lets a user widget write only to `LuaUI/Config/` and `LuaUI/Widgets/`, and lets `pause` and `setspeed` through `SendCommands` ([barwidgets.lua](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/luaui/barwidgets.lua#L605-L632)). Every API the dump needs is registered in the engine: `WorldToScreenCoords`, `GetVisibleUnits`, `GetTeamResources`, `GetFactoryCommands`, `GetUnitCommands` and `IsPosInLos` ([LuaUnsyncedRead.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Lua/LuaUnsyncedRead.cpp); [LuaSyncedRead.cpp](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/rts/Lua/LuaSyncedRead.cpp)). Free metal spots come from `WG.resource_spot_finder.metalSpotsList` ([api_resource_spot_finder.lua](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/common/upgets/api_resource_spot_finder.lua#L395-L424)). The widget should detect events itself and pause on the same frame. Events include an enemy coming into view or a jump in enemy count, a unit or building lost, a build or order that failed, an idle constructor or factory, resources overflowing or stalling, a plan step finished, and the commander's HP falling. Pausing in the widget removes adb and model latency from the AI's reaction time. The one limit is that a widget cannot pause before frame 1 ([barwidgets.lua](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/luaui/barwidgets.lua#L3290-L3330)). The dump should be a diff since the last call, with units grouped by type and position and lists capped. OSWorld-Human found that adding a full accessibility tree puts thousands of tokens into every step and sharply raises latency ([arXiv 2506.16042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16042)). Coordinates need one conversion: the engine draws on an X screen capped at 2100×900's pixel count and 1080 lines, which SGSR then upscales onto the display ([CLAUDE.md](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/bar-droid-app/CLAUDE.md)). The exporter must therefore apply LorieView's scale and offset, or every tap drifts. The app already writes control rectangles for `RtsTouch` (the camera knob and rails, the PLACE panel, the dock's ping rect), and the dump should reuse them rather than recompute them. As a player, the visibility APIs should report only what our ally team sees. That is not yet tested, so check the dump once against a spectator replay of the same game.

**Step 4 turns Claude's macros into touch.** Following Voyager's skill library and PLAP's parameterized skills in microRTS ([arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291); [arXiv 2509.13127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13127)), each macro becomes a laptop script that checks its precondition, performs the touches and confirms the result from the next dump. Examples: "build X at world point P", "queue N of X on repeat", "fight-move group G to P", "Area Mex around the commander", "walls around this sentry", "STRAFE preset to P". The core helper is the equivalent of Pokémon's Navigator: "touch world point (x, z)". It moves the camera by touch (knob, rails, pinch or minimap) until the point is on screen, then taps where `WorldToScreenCoords` says it is. A sequence aborts at the first failed step instead of firing the rest at stale coordinates. Removing that behaviour cost Minitap 12 points ([arXiv 2602.07787](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07787v1)). Reflex rules (keep factories producing, send idle constructors to assist, re-queue a failed build, pull back hurt groups) also run as touch skills; otherwise the UX log misses most of the game's interactions.

**Steps 5–7 make the run measurable and survivable.** The strategist should aim at tens of calls per game, in HIMA's style. Its strategy memory (posture, priority unit, next build) is re-injected every call, as MASMP does, and it takes images only when the dump cannot answer a question. Resilience matters because the dev phone has 8 GB and runs BAR in the app's low-memory mode. Its setup note from 2026-10-29 records that **about 2 in 3 five-minute skirmishes ran through** ([CLAUDE.local.md](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/bar-droid-app/CLAUDE.local.md)), so a 40-minute scenario is at risk unless that has since improved. Saving is blocked only in multiplayer, so singleplayer games can save on a timer ([barwidgets.lua](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/luaui/barwidgets.lua#L605-L632)). The proposed run order is a harness shakedown against a passive opponent (the engine bundles NullAI ([.gitmodules](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/blob/master/.gitmodules)); otherwise BARb Easy), then New Beginning, then BARb Easy, Medium and Hard. One possible pass rule is two wins out of three per tier, for the owner to set.

## Exact coordinates test execution; noise and blind probes test the rest

An agent that knows every button's rectangle can still show whether a finger-sized touch at that spot works. It cannot show whether a person would find the button, read it, or hit it while rushed. Each blind spot needs its own countermeasure and metric:

| What exact coordinates hide | Countermeasure | Metric logged |
|---|---|---|
| Fat-finger misses | Sample each touch point from a 2D Gaussian a few mm wide; taps of about 60–150 ms with slight movement | Hit rate under finger noise, per control |
| Small or crowded targets | Check exported rectangles against 48 dp size and 8 dp spacing (about 130 px on this phone at FHD+; confirm with `wm density`) | Controls under size or spacing |
| Gestures misclassified | Log `RtsTouch`'s decision (tap, pan, box, pinch, cancelled, which button) beside the intent | Reclassification rate per gesture type |
| Discoverability and legibility | Scheduled blind probes: a flow done from screenshots alone, with no rectangles | Steps, confusions, recoveries per flow |
| Time pressure | Unpaused 1× windows in fights | Wall time from intent to completion; touches and retries per intent |
| Agent error versus UI flaw | Count only failures that recur at the same control across games | Recurring-failure list for Kailil to confirm |
| Phone shape | Some games at the Fold7's shapes via `wm size`, as the dock test already does | Findings tagged by screen shape; sizes in mm |

The finger model rests on human-factors work. Google recommends targets of at least **48×48 dp (about 9 mm) with 8 dp spacing** ([Android developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)) and a physical size of 7–10 mm ([Google Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). FFitts law separates a finger's absolute imprecision from the speed-accuracy trade-off, which is why "the centre works" says little ([FFitts law, CHI 2013](https://research.google/pubs/ffitts-law-modeling-finger-touch-with-fitts-law/)). The 60–150 ms tap duration is an estimate built from reported ranges of 100–300 ms for some users and a long-press cut-off near 500 ms ([arXiv 1402.1036](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.1036)). Perfetto's input tracing is documented for userdebug builds with `adb root` ([AOSP Winscope](https://source.android.com/docs/core/graphics/winscope/capture/adb)), so on the retail dev phone the touch layer's own log is the trace. Each row should carry the intended target, the touch points sent and their timing, `RtsTouch`'s classification, the game's response (order issued, selection changed), and a verdict: hit, miss, wrong target or gesture conflict. Blind probes borrow Cradle's finding that unfamiliar icons and non-standard layouts confuse screen-reading agents ([arXiv 2403.03186](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03186)). When a probe gets confused, that is a UX candidate. Every agent-logged finding stays a candidate until a human confirms it, because published agent-UX work still validates against experts ([UXCascade](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.15777)). A useful side effect is that the low-APM strategy keeps sending the agent to the app's automation features (Area Mex, walls, presets, AUTO RETREAT, QUEUE, PLACE shapes, factory repeat), so the controls that most need a stress test get the heaviest use.

## New Beginning is an anti-air race, BARb a race for metal

### New Beginning: survive the first Wasps, then break factories, not the ring

The scenario file defines the task. It is `scenario021.lua` (id Begin04) on Death Valley, Armada only, with a 40-minute par and a perceived difficulty of 8.1 at Normal. **Victory is "Kill all enemy factories"**, and the enemy loses under `deathmode = builders` ([scenario021.lua](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/toolchain/tmp/scen/scenario021.lua)). We start in the south-west with the commander, a 750 E/s Cloakable Fusion, a jammer and three Advanced Metal Extractors. The enemy starts with no commander and no mobile constructors, but with five factories in the north and north-east. One is an Advanced Aircraft Plant assisted by four Construction Turrets with a SAM beside it, and one is a Naval Hovercraft Platform in the water. A T2 ring stands in front of them: 18 Bulwarks, 20 Scorpions, 6 Overseer plasma shields and all 4 Birdshot flak guns. **The factories themselves are covered mostly by T1 Thistles and one SAM** ([scenario021.lua](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/toolchain/tmp/scen/scenario021.lua)). Under BARb's hard profile, that aircraft plant's early output is 40% Wasp gunships ([BARb hard factory.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/master/luarules/configs/BARb/stable/config/hard/factory.json)). A Wasp has 1,560 HP, speed 159 and 111 DPS ([corape](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/corape)). Four of them kill the 3,700 HP commander in about 8 s, and they arrive about 50 s after leaving a plant roughly 7,860 elmos away. That explains the commander deaths at minutes 2–5. The commander's only AA is the Nettle ([armcom.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/master/units/armcom.lua)). A T1 constructor's Chainsaw is the real answer: 750 metal, range 1,200, 625 DPS and 4,450 HP, enough to kill a Wasp in about 2.5 s ([armcir](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armcir)).

| Phase (approximate) | Goal | Touch skills exercised | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame 1, paused | The commander queues 2–3 Nettles beside itself and the fusion, then a Bot Lab with constructors on repeat; the commander stays put | build grid, PLACE with QUEUE, factory repeat | queues confirmed in the dump |
| 0:30–5:00 | First constructors ring the commander and fusion with Chainsaws; a Construction Turret at the lab; auto-pause on the first enemy aircraft | build at a point, assist, event pause | first air wave dead, commander's HP holding |
| 5–15 min | Economy (fusion surplus into up to 6 Advanced Energy Converters), T2 lab, Arbalest flak, radar; optional: the commander captures a stray Cortex constructor | Area Mex, PLACE shapes, select by type | income steady, no AA gaps |
| 15–40 min | Kill every factory: a missile/rocket group behind a T2 front, one fight order at a time; bombers along the map edges against Thistle-covered factories; a separate answer for the hover platform in the water | group select, fight-move, presets, AUTO RETREAT | no enemy factory left, including any new ones |

Several details shape the later phases. Plasma attacks into the ring lose much of their damage to the six Overseers. The Keeper description says BAR shields block plasma artillery but not missiles or bombs ([armgate](https://www.beyondallreason.info/unit/armgate)), so the Ambassador, described as "good vs. static defense" ([BAR units.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/master/language/en/units.json)), and bombers are the natural siege tools. That also makes the briefing's "Build a Shield" tip a mid-game defence against artillery, not an answer to Wasps. Our bombers cannot be repaired, because the air repair pad is capped at 0, and they face Nighthawk fighters at 20% of the aircraft plant's early mix. Nukes, the Basilica and the Vulcan are disabled too ([scenario021.lua](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/toolchain/tmp/scen/scenario021.lua)). Only commanders and Decoy Commanders can capture, so the briefing's "capture enemy constructor" means the commander, with capture speed 1,800 and build distance 145 ([armcom.lua](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/master/units/armcom.lua)). That is optional, and risky with the real commander. The commander's cloak costs 100 E/s standing still against the fusion's 750, so a still, cloaked commander is affordable. Whether BARb can still target it is untested, and the agent should test it early. Human strategy for this scenario is thin: only YouTube titles could be read, for example [Requiem's guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o5mLhGeHcJw), so the plan above is built from the file and the unit data rather than from a proven walkthrough.

### BARb Easy, Medium and Hard: out-expand first, fight with static AA and one big group

BARb's profiles are separate config sets in the game archive, and they differ by how fast and how far the AI grows. Resource bonuses come only from lobby handicaps, and global vision is an option that is off by default ([BAR BARb configs](https://api.github.com/repos/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/contents/luarules/configs/BARb/stable/config); [AIOptions.lua](https://github.com/rlcevg/CircuitAI/blob/barbarian/data/AIOptions.lua)).

Hard's AA rule is the clearest low-APM lever: static AA needs no micro and, by the config's own comment, switches off the enemy's air production. How BARb computes that threat, and how many Chainsaws reach 500, is unknown, so the agent should count them by watching the game. The economy follows BAR's official ratio: about 2 constructors or 1 Construction Turret (200 build power) for every 100 E/s and every 5 M/s ([BAR economy guide](https://beyondallreason.info/guide/in-depth-look-at-economy)). Long factory repeat queues and queued constructor orders keep the base working while Claude is not being called. That is AgileThinker's "default action", carried by the engine. Fights should be a single large group given a single fight order rather than raids, because micro is where every LLM agent above still loses. Community reports support out-building the AI. One player claiming a 100% win rate against 1v1 Hard says BARb "does not have much of a plan" and can be out-ecoed. Another notes it is tuned for semi-flat ground maps and plays oddly elsewhere ([r/beyondallreason](https://redlib.hyperreal.coffee/r/beyondallreason/comments/1hggze1/how_to_be_faster); [r/beyondallreason](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/beyondallreason/comments/1lixzjq/best_map_to_challenge_a_beginner)). A semi-flat 1v1 map is therefore the fair test of a win. First-attack timings for each profile were not found, so the Easy games should measure and log them.

## Open decisions and unknowns to settle on the dev phone

**Open question for the owner: does a win at the scenario's Beginner difficulty count as completing New Beginning?** Beginner adds 50% to the player's resources and leaves the AI and its defences unchanged. Novice adds 25%, Normal is even, and Hard and Brutal only add 25% or 50% to the enemy ([scenario021.lua](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/toolchain/tmp/scen/scenario021.lua)). A Beginner run is a useful harness shakedown either way, but whether it closes the milestone is not this report's decision. The scenario's "Hard" is also not BARb Hard. The start script passes no AI options, and the bundled AIOptions defaults `profile` to `hard` while listing only `dev` as an active item ([AIOptions.lua](file:///C:/Tools/BARANDROID/hailmary-work/recoil-0903/AI/Skirmish/BARb/data/AIOptions.lua)). The four profiles' `init.as` files differ only in their log line ([hard/init.as](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/Beyond-All-Reason/blob/50bd47b34fcbcdd73e2403441cdf62e90a070e26/luarules/configs/BARb/stable/script/hard/init.as)), so one look at the infolog of a New Beginning start settles which profile actually runs.

Five further questions need a measurement before the agent depends on them. First, whether Construction Turrets count as builders under `deathmode = builders`, which decides if killing the factories ends the game. Second, whether a loaded save keeps the scenario's victory check working. Third, whether 40-minute games survive the dev phone's memory. Fourth, whether the exporter really stops at fog of war. Fifth, whether the converted rectangles match where `RtsTouch` places touches on screen, in both layouts and at the Fold7 shapes. The plan also assumes that pause and game speed, issued by the dev widget, count as the harness's clock rather than game actions, which keeps the touch-only rule about orders. If the owner disagrees, pause would need its own touch control.

## Conclusion

The touch dock becomes an action API, and its quality can be measured in touches, retries and misclassifications per macro. Once the harness exists, that measurement can be repeated on every build, so the agent works as a regression benchmark for the touch controls as well as a player. Winning and UX testing reinforce each other here, because a low-APM agent beats BARb by leaning on the app's own automation (factory repeat, Area Mex, walls, presets, AUTO RETREAT). Those features then get exercised harder than a casual human would exercise them. The literature also shifts where the effort should go. Model quality decides only the top tier, while the interface decides whether an agent wins at all, so most of the effort belongs in the exporter's coordinate conversion and the skill library's self-checks, not in prompt craft.

The stated order, New Beginning before the skirmish ladder, puts the hardest problem first. The scenario starts with a fully assisted T2 air factory aimed at the commander, while BARb Easy deliberately avoids early raids. Expect New Beginning to take more attempts than Easy or Medium, and treat those failures as the cheapest source of anti-air and survival skills for Hard. The largest practical risk is not the agent's judgement but whether the 8 GB dev phone can hold a 40-minute game, which is why saves and resumes belong in the first working version rather than in a later polish pass.
